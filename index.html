The gap

A proof is a picture of the job, not the job.

It shows the art on a tilted product render, at the wrong scale, in the wrong colour space, with the imprint area drawn as a dashed box nobody can select. Getting from that to a file your press accepts is manual work, and it happens on every single order.

By hand

  • Open the proof, hunt for the imprint box
  • Trace it, or eyeball the dimensions from the callout
  • Isolate the art, strip the product mockup behind it
  • Scale to actual size and hope the callout was right
  • Convert colour, build a white underbase, choke it
  • Minutes per file, and a different result per operator

With PressReady

  • Drop the proof in
  • The square imprint box is found and locked automatically
  • Skewed boxes on the product view are rejected, not used
  • Art comes out at exactly 1:1
  • Underbase, spot channel, and choke are already correct
  • Seconds, and identical every time

How it works

It reads the proof the way your art lead does.

The hard part was never the export. It was finding the imprint area reliably across every layout a distributor invents, including the ones where the same box appears twice.

Find every candidate box

Dashed borders, solid stroked rectangles, and separate hundred percent artwork pages are all recognised.

Throw out the ones drawn in perspective

Proofs repeat the imprint box on the tilted product render, where it is skewed into a parallelogram. Picking that one is what makes art come out crooked, so only boxes square to the page survive.

Lift the art, drop the mockup

Background panels standing in for product colour, and fills close enough to white to be page rather than ink, are left behind.

Write what the machine wants

Spot channels, choked underbase, pure K vector, or an arc warp, depending on where the job is headed.
